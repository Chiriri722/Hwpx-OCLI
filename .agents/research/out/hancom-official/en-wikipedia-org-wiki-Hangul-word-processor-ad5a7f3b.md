**Hangul** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language "Korean language"): 한글[South Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea") company [Hancom Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hancom "Hancom") Hangul's specialized support for the [Korean written language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language "Korean language") has gained it widespread use in South Korea, especially by the government. Hancom has published their HWP binary format specification online for free.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul_\(word_processor\)#cite_note-1)
The software's name is derived from the Korean word [Hangul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul "Hangul") for the alphabet used to write Korean. In Korean, the software's name is officially stylised (ᄒᆞᆫ글 _arae a_ _a_ _hangul_ _**Arae-A Hangŭl**_ 아래아한글 _**Han/gŭl**_ 한/글[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul_\(word_processor\)#cite_note-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul_\(word_processor\)#cite_note-3)
Haansoft was on the verge of bankruptcy after the release of its 2002 version, due to the widespread use of illegal copies. A campaign to support the development of Korean software and promote the purchase of legal copies of Hangul allowed Haansoft to recover.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul_\(word_processor\)#cite_note-4)
